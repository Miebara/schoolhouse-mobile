# Part One — The Machine That Makes Gods

---

## 1. The Universe Was Not Built For You

### *The Swimmer's Body Illusion*

Professional swimmers have magnificent bodies. It is tempting to conclude that
swimming produces magnificent bodies. Dobelli's correction is blunt: elite
swimmers are not shaped that way *because* they swim well; they swim well
because they are shaped that way. The sport did not sculpt them. It selected
them. Harvard graduates are impressive not in proportion to what Harvard added
but in proportion to how hard it is to get in. Confuse a selection filter with
a cause and you will spend money on the wrong thing for the rest of your life.

This single error, once you see it, dismantles the most sophisticated argument
for God on offer.

The argument is called fine-tuning, and it is genuinely the best one. It goes
like this. The universe runs on a handful of physical constants — the strength
of gravity, the mass of the electron, the cosmological constant, the ratio that
governs how hydrogen fuses into helium. Change some of them by a fraction of a
percent and you get no stars, no carbon, no chemistry, no water, no cells, no
you. The window that permits life appears to be extraordinarily narrow. A
narrow window implies a marksman. Therefore: God.

It is a good argument in the sense that it is not stupid. It is a bad argument
in the sense that it is the swimmer's body illusion wearing a cosmologist's
jacket.

Consider what is doing the observing. Any being capable of noticing that the
constants permit life is, necessarily, a being that the constants permitted. A
universe with the wrong gravity does not contain a disappointed physicist
writing up the negative result. It contains nothing that writes. So the
observation "the constants permit life" is not a discovery about how the
constants were set. It is a logical consequence of there being an observer at
all. You are not the target of the marksman. You are the swimmer — the thing
that got selected, now inferring a cause from your own selection.

Douglas Adams put it better than any philosopher. Imagine a puddle waking up
one morning and marvelling at the hole it sits in: an astonishing fit, every
contour matched, surely made for me. The puddle has the causality backwards,
and it has it backwards in exactly the way Dobelli's swimmers do. The hole did
not accommodate the water. The water took the shape of the hole.

Notice what this argument does *not* require. I do not need multiple universes,
inflationary cosmology, or any speculative physics to make the point. People
often think the anthropic reply depends on the multiverse and dismiss it on
those grounds. It does not. The reply is logical, not physical: an observation
that could only have been made under condition X is not evidence that X was
arranged. This is the same reason you cannot establish that a lottery is rigged
in your favour by noting, after winning, how unlikely your ticket was. Somebody
holds the winning ticket in every draw, and it is always that person who finds
the coincidence remarkable.

The everyday version of the illusion runs through religious life constantly,
usually about Earth rather than the cosmos. Look how perfectly suited this
planet is to us — the temperature, the atmosphere, the tilt that gives us
seasons. But we are not suited to Earth by coincidence; we are the residue of
four billion years of ruthless filtering *by* Earth. Ninety-nine percent of all
species that have ever existed are extinct, most of them killed by this
exquisitely hospitable planet. Three-quarters of the planet's surface would kill
you in an afternoon. We fit our environment the way water fits a hole, and for
the same unremarkable reason.

Then there is the personal-scale version, which is where it bites hardest. A
man survives a car crash that killed the other three passengers and concludes
that he was spared for a purpose. His survival is real, his gratitude is
appropriate, and his inference is the puddle's inference. He is reasoning from
inside the filter. The three who did not survive cannot draw conclusions about
divine intent, so every conclusion drawn about divine intent in that crash is
drawn by a survivor. That is not evidence of a plan. It is a description of who
is available to speak — which is the subject of the next chapter.

Two honest concessions, because this book will be worth nothing if it only
scores points.

First, the anthropic reply does not prove there is no designer. It removes the
*evidential force* of fine-tuning; it does not establish the negative. What it
does is return the question to where it started, with no evidence on either
side — and a claim with no evidence in its favour is not a claim you are obliged
to hold.

Second, physicists genuinely disagree about how impressive the fine-tuning is.
Some of the constants may turn out to be derivable rather than free parameters;
some of the "narrow windows" have been calculated by varying one constant while
holding the others fixed, which is a bit like proving a car cannot run by
draining the oil and leaving everything else alone. I am not going to lean on
that literature, because I do not need it. Even granting the fine-tuning at its
most dramatic, the inference fails at the level of logic, not physics.

There is one more turn of the screw, and it belongs to Dobelli's insistence
that you should ask what a piece of evidence would look like if the opposite
were true. Suppose the constants had been *badly* suited to life — suppose the
universe were 99.99999 percent lethal void, with matter clumping into
radioactive furnaces separated by distances no living thing could ever cross,
and life clinging to a single wet rock for a geological eyeblink before being
sterilised. That is, of course, the universe we actually live in. It has been
described from the pulpit for two thousand years as a testament to design. A
hypothesis compatible with any observation whatsoever is not being tested by
observations. Hold that thought for Chapter 8.

**In conclusion:** whenever you meet an argument of the form *look how well X
fits Y, therefore Y was arranged for X*, ask which one of them was selected by
the other. If the observer could not have existed under any other conditions,
the conditions are not evidence of intent — they are the price of admission.
The universe was not built for you. You were built, painstakingly and
wastefully, by it.
