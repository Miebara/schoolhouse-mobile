# Notes on Sources and Verification

This book is written for the general reader, so it carries no
footnotes. It does carry an obligation, which is to say plainly
where its facts come from and which of them a publisher should check
against primary documents before anything goes to print.

## Where the facts come from

The documentary spine is public and can be checked by anybody.

It consists of the final reports of accident investigators wherever
those reports exist, including the Saudi and Canadian records on
Flight 2120 and the Nigerian reports on ADC 086, EAS 4226, Sosoliso
1145, ADC 053, Dana 992, and the charter loss of 2013.

It consists of the audit results of the International Civil Aviation
Organization, the assessment records of the American Federal
Aviation Administration, and the decisions behind the European air
safety list.

It consists of the Civil Aviation Acts of 2006 and 2022 and the
legislation that established the safety investigation bureau.

It consists of records of the National Assembly, including the
committee examination of the Nigeria Air project.

It consists of the Aviation Safety Network database and the accident
digests of ICAO, used for cross checking dates, registrations, and
figures.

And it consists of the archives of the Nigerian and international
press, without which large parts of this history would not exist in
any retrievable form at all.

## What must be verified before publication

Five categories of fact in this book are stated as accurately as the
public record allows and still require checking against primary
documents. They are flagged here rather than buried.

The first is casualty figures where sources conflict. Nigerian
counts differ between newspapers, official statements, and final
reports, and the disagreement is widest at Ejigbo in 1992, at
Gwammaja in 2002, and for the deaths on the ground at Iju Ishaga in
2012. Where a final report exists this book follows it. Where none
exists this book says so.

The second is the settlement figures and payment tranches in the
saga of the former staff and retirees of Nigeria Airways. The number
of about forty five billion naira, the approval in 2017 and 2018,
and the tranche payments from 2018 onward are drawn from
contemporaneous reporting and from statements by the associations
and by government. The totals actually disbursed, the number of
verified beneficiaries, and the residual claims should all be
checked against ministry and pension records.

The third is the corporate chronology of the successor carriers.
The dates and structure of Nigeria Global around 2001, the launch
and unwinding of Virgin Nigeria between 2004 and 2012, and the
sequence of suspensions and revivals in the Nigeria Air project
between 2016 and 2023 are drawn from press records and legislative
proceedings, and the corporate registry entries should be consulted
directly.

The fourth is the status of legal and administrative proceedings
reported after 2023 in connection with projects of the aviation
ministry. Those matters were unresolved when this book was written.
This book records that they were reported and takes no position on
them, and any edition published later must update the status.

The fifth is every figure in this book that is introduced by the
words about, roughly, or in the order of. Those words are doing real
work. They mark quantities that the public record supports only as
an order of magnitude, including the size of the workforce of
Nigeria Airways at liquidation, the count of carriers licensed since
deregulation, the national burn bed capacity, and the traffic
propensity comparisons in Chapter 17.

## A note on the people in these pages

Every named public figure in this book appears performing documented
actions and making documented statements. Nothing has been placed in
the mouth of a real person.

Where this book describes what somebody in a cockpit, a tower, or a
ministry was thinking, it is reconstructing from the documented
record, from the findings of investigators, and from the published
accounts of people who held those jobs. Those passages are
inferences and the reader should read them as such.

## A note on the argument

The thesis of this book is that aviation crises in Nigeria were not
isolated accidents but symptoms of deeper institutional, political,
and regulatory failure. That is a claim about causation, and
causation in history cannot be proved the way an engineering
conclusion can be proved.

What can be shown is the pattern, and the pattern is unusually clean
here, because Nigeria ran the same system twice under different
rules. Same weather. Same geography. Largely the same professionals
and much of the same fleet. Weak oversight before 2006 and funded,
tenured, published oversight after it. The accident record moved.

Readers who want to test the argument should test it there.
