# Loop C — Source Integration Plan

For each chapter: primary sources, secondary sources, data sources, and
interview targets, with a note on **what each provides** and **how it is
used** (narrative / evidence / comparison / argument).

Legend: (N) narrative material · (E) evidence/facts · (C) comparison ·
(A) argument/framing.

## Chapter 11 — The Regulator's Dilemma
Primary: ICAO USOAP audit history for Nigeria; Civil Aviation Acts 2006 &
2022; National Assembly aviation committee hearing records. Secondary:
regulatory capture theory (Stigler; Carpenter & Moss, *Preventing Regulatory
Capture*); FAA/737 MAX oversight reports (for the comparison that capture is
universal — outcomes differ by architecture); UK CAA and SACAA institutional
studies. Data: NCAA leadership tenure table (constructed); audit scores over
time. Interviews: former DGs of the NCAA; ministers of aviation; airline
owners. Use: A/E throughout; the tenure table is the chapter's spine.

## Chapter 12 — The Economics of Cutting Corners
Primary: AOC registry and airline failure ledger (constructed appendix);
CBN forex policy circulars affecting spares. Secondary: aircraft leasing &
Cape Town Convention literature; AFRAA/IATA African aviation economics
reports; US deregulation safety literature (Levine, Borenstein). Data:
fleet-age distributions Nigeria vs. Ethiopia/Kenya/South Africa; insurance
premium data. Interviews: lessors, airline CFOs, insurers, MRO managers.
Use: E for the arithmetic, C for the financing contrast, A for "safety is
purchased upstream."

## Chapter 13 — When Help Doesn't Come
Primary: ICAO Annex 14 ARFF categories; AIB survival-factors sections
(Bellview, Sosoliso, Dana); NEMA statutory reports. Secondary: emergency
management scholarship (Comfort, Tierney); golden-hour trauma literature;
US airport disaster-drill regime; Hudson ditching accounts. Data: response
time reconstructions; burn-unit capacity. Interviews: FAAN fire service
veterans; NEMA officials; trauma surgeons. Use: N for the three response
narratives, E for standards-vs-reality, A for "response is the most
tractable reform."

## Chapter 14 — The Culture of Silence
Primary: ICAO Annex 13; ledger of AIB/NSIB reports published vs. pending
(constructed appendix). Secondary: Sidney Dekker, *Just Culture*; NTSB
public docket system; BEA/French judicial tension literature; Danish/NZ
just-culture statutes. Interviews: investigators, prosecutors, victims'
family associations. Use: the publication ledger is the spine (E); Dekker
supplies the conceptual frame (A); NTSB/BEA the comparison (C).

## Chapter 15 — Lessons from Elsewhere
Primary: EU air safety list decisions & criteria; ICAO USOAP comparative
scores; FAA IASA categories over time. Secondary: Ethiopian Airlines
histories; Kenya Airways/KCAA studies; SACAA record; Indonesian DGCA reform
documentation. Data: accident rates per departure by country over decades
(ASN + departure data). Use: five country mirrors (C), each closing on what
transfers to Nigeria (A).

## Chapter 16 — Rebuilding Trust
Primary: Civil Aviation Act 2022; NSIB Act; Cape Town Convention compliance
index; SAATM protocols; Nigeria Air procurement record & court filings.
Secondary: airport corporatization case studies (GACA, Kenya, South Africa);
military airworthiness reform (UK MAA). Use: the ten-point reform agenda
(A) with each point evidenced (E) and benchmarked (C).

## Chapter 17 — A Nigerian Century in the Air?
Primary: Airbus GMF & Boeing CMO Africa forecasts; UN World Population
Prospects; AfDB/AU infrastructure and SAATM documents. Use: forward-looking
synthesis (A); the demographic and market data (E) set the stakes; Ethiopia
and Indonesia trajectories (C) frame the choice.

## Cross-cutting verification tasks (pre-publication)

1. Verify every date, flight number, registration, and casualty figure
   against the final accident report where one exists, ASN otherwise.
2. Confirm publication status of each AIB/NSIB report cited (the Chapter 14
   ledger must be current at press time).
3. Fact-check all named-person statements against at least two independent
   contemporaneous sources; obtain right-of-reply where criticism is direct.
4. Clear quotation permissions; where interviews are unobtainable, convert
   passages to reported (non-quoted) form.
