# The Art of Doubting Clearly

### How Ordinary Thinking Errors Build a God and Fill a Church

A short book in twenty-seven chapters, written in the format of Rolf Dobelli's
*The Art of Thinking Clearly*: one thinking error per chapter, a concrete case,
the mechanism, the application, and a closing piece of practical advice.

The argument is cumulative. Part One shows that the machinery of ordinary human
cognition manufactures gods whether or not any exist. Part Two takes the
standard arguments for belief and shows that each one is a named, catalogued
reasoning error. Part Three turns to the institution and shows that a church
service is, structurally, a bias amplifier. Part Four is the honest part: what
the pew genuinely delivers, which biases unbelievers fall for, and what to
actually do on Sunday morning.

## A note on sources

Rolf Dobelli's *The Art of Thinking Clearly* (2011 in German, 2013 in English)
is the organising spine of this book: ninety-nine short chapters, each on one
systematic error in human reasoning. The errors themselves are not Dobelli's
inventions — they belong to the research literature, mostly to Kahneman,
Tversky, Cialdini, Festinger, Asch, Aronson, Taleb and their successors — but
the framing used here is his: that these errors are *predictable*, that knowing
them does not make you immune, and that the practical remedy is usually
negative, a matter of subtracting error rather than adding conviction.

Nothing in this book quotes Dobelli at length. Where a chapter borrows one of
his examples or the name he gives an error, it says so.
